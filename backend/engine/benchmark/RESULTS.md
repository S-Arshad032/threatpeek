# Benchmark results

Generated by `npm run benchmark -- --set=<dev|heldout|heldout2|heldout3|heldout4|heldout5>` in `backend/engine`.
These are small, synthetic, English-only test sets; the numbers are not a guarantee of real-world
accuracy. dev, heldout and heldout2 were written by the same author with fictional brands and reserved
domains. The dev set was used to tune the rules; the held-out sets were not.

heldout3 (30 messages, 15 scam / 15 benign) was written by a third party (Gemini). It was not seen
while the round 1 rules were written. **heldout3 is now seen, used for rule tuning in round 2**: its
misses guided lookalike-lure-domain, utility-disconnection-threat, payment-failed-lure,
account-security-lure, the job-fee floor and the remote-work task-job path. Its round 2 numbers below
are therefore optimistic and are not a held-out measure. The rules are general (no sentence from
heldout3 is in a rule or a test). Round 1 result, measured before it was seen: 60.0% detection (9/15),
13.3% strong, 0.0% false alarms. It uses real Bangladeshi service names (bKash, Nagad, DESCO...) and
throwaway defanged domains. Known limitation: 3 romanized-Bengali messages in the draft were replaced
by English versions, so the set does not measure mixed-language messages. h3-s06 (scam: "my main
number got blocked... urgent 3,000 BDT bKash") and h3-b13 (benign: "my card got declined... send me
600 BDT on bKash") are near-twins that a rule engine cannot reliably separate; h3-s06 is still missed.

heldout4 (30 messages, 15 scam / 15 benign) was written by a third party (Gemini) and covers different
scam types than all earlier sets (KYC and SIM re-verification, scholarship, visa and loan fees, rental
deposits, fake charity, marketplace QR codes, changed bank details on an invoice, page appeals,
government grants, verification badges). **First contact, before round 3** (2026-11-27, not seen while
any rule was written, measured without changing a rule): 13.3% detection (2/15), 13.3% scored 50+,
0.0% strong, 6.7% false alarms (1/15: a real "your password was changed" bank alert). That is the
honest unseen result. **heldout4 is now seen after round 3 (used for tuning)**: its misses and false
alarm guided the credential-request fix (a request verb is needed, not a notice) and the
unfamiliar-link-pressure signal, so its section below is optimistic and not a held-out measure. No
sentence from heldout4 is in a rule or a test. Its 7 scams without a link (rental deposit, wrong-number
investor, charity, fake antivirus call, marketplace QR, invoice bank change, visa bond) are still missed.

heldout5 is reserved and empty, kept for 30 fresh messages that nobody has seen. It is the next fully
unseen set; until it is filled there is no unseen measure of the round 3 rules. All sets are synthetic
and English-only; the numbers are not a guarantee.
